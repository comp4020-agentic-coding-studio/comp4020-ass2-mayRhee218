## The brief

> Pick one week's drama and one claim about it, and argue that claim closely
> enough that a reader who hasn't seen the show can follow your evidence.

By week 7 you'll have practised this course's central move --- separating what a
drama represents from what it dramatises --- six times, on six different
subjects. This essay asks you to do it once, in depth, on a single drama of
your choosing from weeks 1 through 7 (or by arrangement, a later week you've
previewed).

Choose a claim you can actually support in the space you have: not "this drama
is about Korean culture," but something specific enough to be wrong about ---
what a particular scene does, what it assumes its audience already believes,
or where the line between record and invention actually falls.

## What you submit

A 1200--1500 word essay, referencing specific scenes or moments (with
timestamps or episode numbers) as evidence for your claim.

## How it's marked

Depth of analysis carries the most weight: does the essay go past the obvious
reading? Evidence from the drama itself matters as much as the argument built
on it, and clarity is what makes the first two visible to a reader.
