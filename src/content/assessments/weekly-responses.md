## The brief

> Watch, react, and say something specific --- not everything, just the one
> thing this week's drama actually made you think about.

Twelve weeks is a lot of television and a lot of territory --- food, politics,
family, gender, work, education, history, memory, class, appearance, and
service. The weekly response is where you keep pace with that, one short
reaction at a time, rather than saving all your thinking for two big essays.

A strong response picks one of the week's questions and pushes on it: a claim,
a piece of evidence from the episode, and a sentence on what it would mean if
you're right. A plot summary with a comment tacked on the end is not a
response.

## What you submit

A short written response (150--300 words) for most weeks of the semester,
submitted by the end of that teaching week. Aim for coverage across the
semester rather than a perfect run --- an occasional missed week costs less
than twelve rushed ones.

## How it's marked

Two criteria: whether each response actually engages with that week's
questions, and whether engagement holds up across the semester rather than
front-loading effort into the first few weeks and coasting after.
