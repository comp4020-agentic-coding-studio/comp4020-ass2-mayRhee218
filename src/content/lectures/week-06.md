Misaeng (미생) --- Incomplete Life --- follows a former professional Go player
turned contract worker at a trading company, and stays remarkably close to the
texture of office life: the meetings, the hierarchy, the hours. It's often
cited by Korean viewers as one of the more accurate workplace dramas made, which
makes it a useful test case for this week's real question --- how do you tell a
genuinely common social pattern apart from a stereotype the genre just repeats?

## Questions

- What does Misaeng reveal about Korean corporate culture specifically, rather
  than about corporate culture in general?
- How do hierarchy, seniority, teamwork, and long working hours interact in the
  show's office?
- How has Korean workplace culture changed since the show was made?
- How should we tell a social pattern apart from a stereotype, when a drama
  presents both with the same confidence?

## What to take from this week

- hierarchy as a structuring feature of the workplace, not just a management
  style
- seniority and what it entitles someone to, formally and informally
- corporate culture as a specific set of Korean institutions, not "how
  everyone works"
- work-life balance, and how the show frames its absence
- employment structures, including the contract-versus-permanent distinction
  the plot turns on
- workplace relationships as a hierarchy people also have to live inside
