Crash Landing on You (사랑의 불시착) strands a South Korean heiress in North
Korea and builds a romance out of the accident. It's the most-watched drama on
this syllabus outside Korea, largely because the premise is legible without
context --- which is also the risk: a division built on a war, a border, and
seven decades of separate development can look, in a drama, like a
misunderstanding waiting to be resolved by two people falling in love.

## Questions

- How has the division of Korea shaped Korean identity, on both sides of the
  border and in the diaspora?
- What differences between North and South Korea does the drama actually
  represent, and which does it flatten?
- What is romanticised or simplified for the sake of the love story?
- How should we separate fiction from historical and political reality when
  the fiction is this persuasive?

## What to take from this week

- the Korean War as the event the division follows from, not a footnote to it
- the division of Korea as an ongoing condition, not a past event
- what's actually different, day to day, north and south of the border
- propaganda and representation, on both sides, and how a drama can reproduce
  either without meaning to
- national identity as something the division itself keeps reshaping
