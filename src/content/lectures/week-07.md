SKY Castle (스카이 캐슬) is a satire of the pressure to get children into Korea's
top three universities (Seoul, Korea, and Yonsei --- the "SKY" of the title),
following wealthy families and the private admissions coordinators they hire.
It's exaggerated by design, but the exaggeration is a useful diagnostic: it
only works as satire because the underlying pressure is real and widely
recognised.

## Questions

- Why is education so socially important in Korea, beyond "parents want their
  children to do well"?
- What role does university entrance play in family life and family status?
- How do private academies and competition shape childhood, in the show and
  outside it?
- What does SKY Castle exaggerate for satire, and what does it capture
  accurately about the pressure underneath?

## What to take from this week

- education as a site where family status and a child's future are both
  understood to be at stake
- university admissions and the weight placed on a small number of
  institutions
- private education / hagwon culture as an industry built around that weight
- social mobility, and education's role as a (contested) route to it
- parental pressure as something the show amplifies rather than invents
- inequality --- who can afford the private coordinators the show centres, and
  who can't
