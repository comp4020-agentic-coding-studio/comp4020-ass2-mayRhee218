New Recruit (신병) follows a young man's first weeks of compulsory military
service, played largely for comedy rather than the grim-training-camp mode the
subject usually gets. That choice of tone is itself informative: national
conscription is such a shared experience for Korean men that a comedy can lean
on its rhythms --- hierarchy, boredom, small humiliations --- as a source of
recognisable jokes rather than trauma, which is one way a mandatory institution
becomes a cultural common ground.

## Questions

- Why does military service occupy such an important place in South Korean
  society?
- What does military culture reveal about hierarchy and masculinity more
  broadly?
- How is compulsory military service represented in popular media, and what
  does a comic register do to that representation?
- How does military experience intersect with everyday Korean life --- at work,
  in friendships, in shared reference points --- after service ends?

## What to take from this week

- conscription as a near-universal, time-bound experience for Korean men,
  shaping expectations before and after it happens
- military hierarchy as a compressed, exaggerated version of hierarchies we've
  traced all semester --- workplace, family, school
- masculinity as something the institution actively produces, not just
  reflects
- social expectations built around having served, or not yet having served
- military service as popular-media subject matter, comic here and grim
  elsewhere
- civilian-military relationships, and how thoroughly the two overlap in
  Korean daily life
