Pokssak Sogatsuda (폭싹 속았수다) --- When Life Gives You Tangerines --- follows a
Jeju Island family across decades, from a hard postwar childhood to the
adulthood of the next generation. Its span is the point: a single household
lets us watch what "family" was expected to mean change underneath people who
didn't necessarily choose the change themselves.

## Questions

- How have Korean family structures changed across the decades the drama
  covers?
- What roles have parents and children traditionally occupied, and where does
  the drama show those roles under strain?
- How does economic development --- migration, new work, new money --- reshape
  family relationships?
- How do different generations in the drama understand duty and independence
  differently from one another?

## What to take from this week

- family as a structure with historically specific, changing expectations
- filial responsibility, and what it has and hasn't meant across generations
- generational difference as a source of both continuity and conflict
- marriage as a family decision as much as an individual one, in the periods
  the drama covers
- changing social expectations, and how quickly a "traditional" norm can turn
  out to be recent
