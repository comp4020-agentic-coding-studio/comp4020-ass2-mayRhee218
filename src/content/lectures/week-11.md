My ID Is Gangnam Beauty (내 ID는 강남미인) follows a student who has undergone
extensive cosmetic surgery starting a new school, and the social response --- both
praise and cruelty --- that follows her there. It's a deliberately uncomfortable
premise, and it puts the show at the centre of a real, ongoing Korean debate
about cosmetic surgery, appearance, and what either is actually for.

## Questions

- Why is appearance such a powerful social topic in contemporary Korea?
- How are beauty standards constructed, and by whom --- media, family, peers?
- What role do media and social comparison play in enforcing those standards?
- How has Korean society itself debated cosmetic surgery, rather than simply
  accepted or rejected it?

## What to take from this week

- beauty standards as constructed and historically specific, not natural or
  fixed
- appearance as a social and economic asset, not only a personal preference
- cosmetic surgery as an industry and a practice with its own Korean history
  and debate
- gender, and how differently appearance pressure lands depending on it
- social pressure as something the show depicts from several directions at
  once, not just one
- media representation's role in setting a standard that then gets treated as
  natural

## Approaching this week

Avoid stereotypes such as "all Koreans get plastic surgery." Cosmetic surgery
rates and attitudes vary considerably by age, region, and social group, and are
themselves debated within Korea --- that debate is the actual subject this week,
not a settled national fact.
