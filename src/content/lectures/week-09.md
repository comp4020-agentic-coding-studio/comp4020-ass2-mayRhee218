Reply 1988 (응답하라 1988) follows five neighbouring families in Seoul through
the last years of the decade, foregrounding ordinary domestic life --- meals,
friendships, small economies --- over the political events happening around it.
That choice is itself worth examining: a show can be historically situated in
an authoritarian, rapidly developing country while barely mentioning either
directly, and the warmth of the memory it recreates raises its own historical
question.

## Questions

- What was ordinary life like under authoritarian rule, for people not
  directly involved in politics?
- How did working-class and middle-class families experience rapid economic
  change in this period?
- What role did neighbourhoods and community play in daily life?
- How does nostalgia affect historical memory --- what does a warm retelling
  make easier to forget?

## What to take from this week

- 1980s Korea as a period of both authoritarian rule and rapid growth,
  simultaneously
- authoritarianism as a backdrop the show mostly declines to foreground
- democratisation, which the period is building toward without the show
  dwelling on it
- family life and its economics under rapid change
- neighbourhood culture as a form of social organisation the show treats as
  central
- economic development as lived experience, not a statistic
- nostalgia, and what a fond memory of a hard decade tends to leave out
