# Coffee Prince
## What a 2000s audience needed to find plausible

---

## Today's drama

**The 1st Shop of Coffee Prince (커피프린스 1호점)** --- a woman disguised as a
man, and the confusion that follows.

Watched now, it's as much a record of what a 2007 Korean audience found
thinkable and safely resolvable as it is a romance.

---

## Three questions

1. How did Korean television represent gender and sexuality in the 2000s?
2. What does the show's gender-disguise premise reveal about the social
   expectations it was written against?
3. What is the difference between a media representation and a lived
   experience --- and why does collapsing the two produce bad arguments?

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> This is about what the drama assumed and represented --- not a verdict on
> a whole society's beliefs.

---

## Take from this week

- what counted as a safe way to raise the subject, in 2007
- what a gender-disguise plot needs its audience to find plausible and
  resolvable
- change over time in attitudes, not one fixed "Korean attitude"
- the gap between representation and lived experience
