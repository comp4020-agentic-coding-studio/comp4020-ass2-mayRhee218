# Mr. Sunshine
## What a historical drama constructs, not just depicts

---

## Today's drama

**Mr. Sunshine (미스터 선샤인)** --- late Joseon, as Japanese colonial
expansion takes hold, and characters drawn into the early independence
movement.

A big-budget, openly patriotic historical drama --- and exactly the thing to
examine this week.

---

## Three questions

1. What forms did Korean resistance actually take, and which of those does
   the drama show?
2. What does the drama remember, simplify, or romanticise about this
   period?
3. How does historical drama, as a genre, construct national memory --- and
   what responsibility does that carry?

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> A compelling scene is not a source. A claim about the colonial period
> needs a historical source behind it, not just a citation of the show.

---

## Take from this week

- the late Korean Empire and the conditions that preceded annexation
- independence movements, in their actual variety --- armed, diplomatic,
  cultural
- resistance as organised, sustained political action, not a single hero's
  arc
- the gap between a national story and the historical record
