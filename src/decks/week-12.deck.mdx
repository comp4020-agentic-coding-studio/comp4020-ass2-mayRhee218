# New Recruit
## A mandatory institution, played for comedy

---

## Today's drama

**New Recruit (신병)** --- a young man's first weeks of compulsory military
service, played for comedy rather than the grim training-camp mode the
subject usually gets.

That choice of tone is itself informative.

---

## Three questions

1. Why does military service occupy such an important place in South
   Korean society?
2. What does military culture reveal about hierarchy and masculinity more
   broadly?
3. How does military experience intersect with everyday Korean life ---
   at work, in friendships, in shared reference points --- after service
   ends?

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> National conscription is such a shared experience for Korean men that a
> comedy can lean on its rhythms as a source of recognisable jokes rather
> than trauma.

---

## Take from this week

- conscription as a near-universal, time-bound experience for Korean men
- military hierarchy as a compressed version of hierarchies we've traced
  all semester --- workplace, family, school
- masculinity as something the institution actively produces, not just
  reflects
- civilian-military relationships, and how thoroughly the two overlap in
  Korean daily life
