# Jewel in the Palace
## Food, medicine, and rank in Joseon Korea

---

## Today's drama

**Daejanggeum (대장금)** --- an orphan girl rises from the palace kitchens to
become the king's personal physician.

A costume epic on the surface. A food-systems documentary underneath, more
than you'd expect.

---

## Two questions, every week

1. What does this get **right** about the period?
2. What does it **invent**, and why?

We'll run this pass on every drama this semester. Today is the first time.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "You are what the palace lets you eat."

The kitchen isn't background --- it's where rank gets enforced.

---

## Royal cuisine as hierarchy

- who is permitted to cook for whom
- who is permitted to *eat* what, and when
- punishment and reward that runs through food, not around it

A meal in this drama is rarely just a meal.

---

## Food as medicine

Joseon medical theory treats food as the first line of treatment ---
not a separate system from cooking, but continuous with it.

Daejanggeum's arc (cook → physician) only makes sense inside that theory.

---

## Historical vs. dramatised

The real Jang-geum appears in the Joseon *Veritable Records* in a handful of
lines. Everything else --- her childhood, her rivalries, her romances --- is the
writers filling the gaps.

**Your job this week:** find one gap, and one thing built to fill it.
