# The Art of Sarah
## Not luxury goods --- what wanting them visibly does

---

## Today's drama

**The Art of Sarah (레이디 두아)** --- a woman turns a debt crisis into a
counterfeit luxury-goods scheme, sold to Korea's upper class and
entertainment industry.

The scam only works because the desire for the genuine article is so
strong that almost no one stops to check.

---

## Three questions

1. Why do wealth and status show up as such visible, legible cultural
   themes in Korean television specifically?
2. What does it mean that almost nobody in the story checks whether the
   goods are genuine?
3. Is this a story about Korea, or a story about status economies more
   generally that happens to be set in Korea?

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> Do not reduce Korean society to "materialistic" --- ask instead why
> wealth and status become such visible, dramatizable cultural themes
> here.

---

## Take from this week

- materialism as a social performance, not just a personal trait
- status and how it's signalled through visible consumption
- wealth as distinct from the appearance of wealth, which is the show's
  actual subject
- social comparison as the engine the scam runs on
